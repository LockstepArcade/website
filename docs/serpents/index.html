Serpents

The Serpents game runs on mobile phones, as well as desktop, and supports single player and multi-player versus game modes.

Game Objectives

In challenge games (single player or online co-op games), the objective is always to reach the exit, you must reach the exit in order to pass each level, and only games where you reached the exit count towards high scores. Depending on the map, high scores are then ranked either by the time taken to exit, or the score amassed at time of exit.

The goal in PvP or team battle matches is simply to amass the highest score by the time the game ends.

Build up your 'tail' by collecting stars (like in the classic 'Snake' game). Be careful not to cross your own tail, or run into anyone else's, or the tail will be destroyed (and you'll have to start building up again, from scratch).

Your score at the end of the game is then composed of:

  • The length of your tail at game end
  • Score banked at a collector (if any)
  • Bounties collected during the game (see below)

Game Controls

Control your serpent by clicking or holding left mouse (on desktop platforms), or touching the screen (on touch screen platforms), in the direction you want your serpent to accelerate towards.

A red circle around the serpent head, at the centre of the screen, shows the control region. Clicking or touching at or outside this circle will give you maximum acceleration and clicking inside the circle then enables more delicate control.

Advice

  • Don't let your speed get out of control. Tapping or clicking directly behind your serpent head is a good way to slow down.
  • Clicking further away from the serpent head applies more acceleration.
  • You can turn around quickly by holding the mouse (or touching and holding) a long way behind the serpent head (e.g. at the edge of the screen).
  • The controls are tricky and take some time to master, but take some time to practice and you will get the hang of this.

Bounties

Bounty zones are shown as shaded gold polygons you can move over.

Bounty zones are announced some time before they activate, to give players a chance to prepare to attempt to collect them.

Once a bounty zone is active, you can collect the bounty by packing a set amount of tail into the bounty region. (The amount required depends on the size of the bounty zone.) When you enter the bounty zone you will see a percentage indicator telling you how close you are and you need to get this indicator to 100% in order to collect.

Trigger Zones

Trigger zones are similar to bounty zones, but these zones are fixed, and open a door instead of providing bounty, when completed.

Trigger zones are shown in blue, and only need to be completed once and then they will disappear.

Threading gates

On some maps there are sequences of gates, that need to be threaded, in order to open a door.

Individual gates light up when your serpent tail is over them, and the whole set of gates must be activated simultaneously in order to activate the trigger.
